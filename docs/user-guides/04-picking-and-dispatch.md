# Picking and Dispatch Guide

**For:** Warehouse supervisors who plan picking waves, pickers who execute pick tasks, and dispatch operatives who prepare and load outbound shipments.

---

## Prerequisites

- Stock must be available in the warehouse (received and put away). See [Receiving Guide](03-receiving.md).
- Sales orders or transfer orders must exist in the system before picking can begin.
- Pick lists are generated by the warehouse supervisor or automatically by the system at scheduled intervals.
- Know which dispatch workflow your warehouse uses: **1-step, 2-step, or 3-step**. Your warehouse manager will confirm this.

---

## Overview

The outbound process moves goods from storage bins to the customer's truck. It follows this sequence:

**Sales Order → Wave Planning → Pick List → Picking → Staging → Dispatch**

- **Wave planning** groups multiple orders together to make picking efficient.
- **Batch picking** means one picker handles multiple orders in a single trip through the warehouse.
- **Staging** is the area where picked goods wait before loading onto the truck.
- **Dispatch** is the final confirmation step that records goods as having left the warehouse.

---

## Section 1: Understanding FIFO and FEFO

Before picking begins, it is important to understand how the system decides which stock to pick when there are multiple batches or pallets of the same product.

### FIFO — First In, First Out

The system always directs pickers to the stock that arrived in the warehouse first. This prevents older stock from being left at the back while newer stock is dispatched.

**In practice:** If you received 10 units of a split AC in January and another 10 units in March, FIFO means the January batch must be picked first. The system enforces this — the pick task will point you to the January stock's bin. You cannot override this and pick from the March bin instead (unless your supervisor overrides on-screen).

### FEFO — First Expired, First Out

For batch-tracked products where an expiry or manufacture date matters (such as lubricants, gas canisters, or components with shelf life), FEFO overrides FIFO. The batch closest to its expiry date is picked first, regardless of when it arrived.

**In practice:** If you have two batches of refrigerant oil and one expires in June while the other expires in September, the June batch will always be directed to pick first.

> **Important:** Do not rearrange stock in the warehouse on your own. If a bin looks disorganised, report it to your supervisor. Moving stock without recording it in the system breaks FIFO/FEFO tracking and can cause picking errors.

## Section 2: Wave Picking Setup

A **wave** is a group of orders released for picking at the same time. Grouping orders into waves makes picking more efficient because multiple orders are fulfilled in a single pass through the warehouse.

### Creating a Wave

1. Go to **Outbound > Wave Planning**.
2. You will see a list of sales orders that are ready to pick (status: Confirmed, stock allocated).
3. To filter orders by criteria before grouping them into a wave, use the filter options:
   - **By Zone**: group all orders that need items from the same zone (e.g., all orders needing items from the Mezzanine zone)
   - **By Product Type**: group all orders containing outdoor units together, and indoor units together
   - **By Delivery Route / Area**: group orders going to the same city or delivery zone
   - **By Urgency**: prioritise orders marked as urgent or with same-day delivery
4. Tick the orders you want to include in this wave and click **Create Wave**.
5. Set a **Wave Name** (e.g., "Wave 01 – Morning – 22 Apr") and click **Confirm**.
6. The system generates pick lists for this wave and assigns them to available pickers.

> **Tip:** A well-planned wave reduces picker travel time significantly. As a rule of thumb, keep each wave to a size that one picker can complete in 60–90 minutes. For large warehouses, run multiple waves in parallel with different pickers assigned to each.

## Section 3: Batch Picking (Multiple Orders in One Trip)

Batch picking lets a single picker handle several orders in one trip through the warehouse, sorting items into separate totes or locations as they go.

1. Go to **Outbound > My Pick Tasks** (on the handheld device or pick screen).
2. The system will show your assigned pick tasks. If you are assigned a batch pick, it will say **"Batch Pick – X Orders"**.
3. Tap **Start Batch Pick**.
4. You will see a consolidated list: one row per product with the total quantity needed across all orders in the batch.
5. The system guides you through the warehouse in an optimised sequence to minimise walking distance.
6. At each bin, scan the bin barcode, scan the product barcode, and enter or confirm the quantity to pick.
7. If you are picking into separate totes (one per order), the screen will tell you which tote to place each unit into. Place the item in the correct tote and scan the tote barcode to confirm.
8. If you are picking into a single trolley (sorted later at staging), simply confirm the quantity picked.

> **Tip:** Using separate totes per order (tote-based batch picking) makes the staging step faster because items are already sorted by order. If your warehouse uses trolleys, expect some additional sorting time at staging.

## Section 4: The Pick Screen — Step by Step

Whether you are doing a single-order pick or a batch pick, the pick screen works the same way.

**The pick screen shows you, for each task:**
- The bin to go to (Aisle – Rack – Bin code)
- The product to pick (name, SKU, barcode)
- The quantity to pick
- The serial numbers to pick (for serialised products)
- The tote or staging area to bring it to

**Completing a pick task step by step:**

1. On your handheld device, open **My Pick Tasks** and tap the first task in the list.
2. Walk to the bin shown on the screen.
3. **Scan the bin barcode** on the rack label. The screen will confirm "Correct Bin".
   - If the screen shows "Wrong Bin", you are at the wrong location. Walk to the correct bin.
4. **Scan the product barcode** on the item you are about to pick. The screen will confirm "Correct Product".
   - If the screen shows "Wrong Product", do not pick that item. Check the bin — there may be a put-away error. Report it to your supervisor.
5. **Enter or confirm the quantity**. For most items, you tap the confirm button if the quantity on-screen is correct. If you are picking fewer than instructed (a short pick), change the number and tap the reason:
   - **Bin Empty** — the bin shows as having stock but it is physically empty
   - **Damaged Stock** — the items in the bin are damaged and cannot be picked
   - **Insufficient Qty** — there is some stock but not enough to fulfil the full pick
6. **For serialised products**: after confirming the quantity, you will be prompted to scan each serial number. Scan them one by one. The system records exactly which serial number went to which order.
7. Tap **Confirm Pick** to complete this task line and move to the next bin.
8. Continue until all lines are picked.
9. Bring the picked goods to the **Staging zone** indicated on the screen.

### Short Picks

If you cannot pick the full quantity (bin empty or damaged stock), the system will:
- Record the shortage against the sales order.
- Notify the supervisor automatically.
- The supervisor can decide to: wait for more stock, split the order for partial dispatch, or source from another warehouse.

## Section 5: Dispatch — 1-Step Workflow

For simple, low-volume dispatch:

1. Go to **Outbound > Ready to Dispatch**.
2. Find the delivery order and click **Dispatch**.
3. Confirm the transporter details (vehicle number, driver name).
4. Click **Confirm Dispatch**. The system records the goods as dispatched and updates stock.
5. A **Delivery Note** and **Invoice** are generated automatically. Print and hand to the driver.

---

## Section 6: Dispatch — 2-Step Workflow

For medium-volume warehouses where a packing or verification step is required:

### Step A: Pack / Stage Verification

1. After picking is complete, go to **Outbound > Staging Tasks**.
2. Open the staging task for this delivery order.
3. At the staging area, verify each item against the pick list:
   - Scan each product barcode.
   - Confirm quantities match the delivery order.
   - If any short picks were recorded, the system shows the adjusted quantities.
4. Pack items into shipping cartons or pallets as needed.
5. Apply the **Delivery Label** (printed from the system) to each carton or pallet.
6. Click **Confirm Staged**.

### Step B: Dispatch

1. Go to **Outbound > Ready to Dispatch**.
2. Find the delivery order (status: Staged).
3. Confirm transporter details.
4. Click **Confirm Dispatch**.
5. Print the Delivery Note and Invoice.

---

## Section 7: Dispatch — 3-Step Workflow

For high-volume or high-value dispatch requiring full documentation:

### Step A: Pick (as described in Section 4)

### Step B: Pack and Quality Gate

1. Go to **Outbound > Packing Tasks**.
2. Open the packing task and verify every item:
   - Scan all products.
   - Check serialised items against the dispatch serial list.
   - Mark any discrepancies.
3. Record packing details: number of cartons, total weight, pallet count.
4. The system generates a **Packing List** — print it and place it inside the lead carton.
5. Apply delivery labels to all cartons.
6. Click **Confirm Packed**. The order status moves to "Ready for Loading".

### Step C: Loading and Dispatch

1. Go to **Outbound > Loading Tasks**.
2. Confirm the assigned truck/vehicle and dock door.
3. As each item is loaded, scan the carton or pallet barcode to record it as loaded.
4. Once all cartons are loaded, click **Confirm Loaded**.
5. Click **Confirm Dispatch**.
6. Print the Delivery Note, Invoice, and Packing List. The driver signs the Delivery Note; keep the signed copy.

## Section 8: Handling Exceptions During Dispatch

**Customer refuses delivery (at the door):**
- The driver brings goods back and reports to the supervisor.
- The supervisor creates a **Sales Return** against the original delivery order. See [Returns Guide](05-returns.md).

**Goods damaged during loading:**
- Stop and report to supervisor immediately.
- Do not dispatch damaged goods.
- Record the damage: go to the delivery order, click **Report Damage**, enter the quantity and description.
- Replace from stock if available, or contact the customer to reschedule.

**Wrong items loaded:**
- If discovered before the truck leaves: reload the correct items; return the wrong items to their bin using an internal transfer task.
- If discovered after dispatch: process a return when goods come back. See [Returns Guide](05-returns.md).

## Tips and Warnings

- **Never pick from a bin that the system has not directed you to.** Even if you can see the correct product in a nearby bin, always follow the system's instruction. Unauthorised picks break FIFO order and stock accuracy.
- **The pick screen timeout is 5 minutes.** If you leave the screen idle for 5 minutes, your task will be released back to the task pool. Simply reopen it from My Pick Tasks.
- **Refrigerant cylinders have a special pick process.** When you pick a cylinder, the system will ask you to confirm the cylinder ID (scanned from the cylinder valve tag) and the current weight. See [Refrigerant Compliance](09-refrigerant-compliance.md).
- **Report empty bins immediately.** If you reach a bin and find it empty when the system shows stock, do not guess. Tap "Bin Empty" and move on. Your supervisor will investigate the discrepancy.
- **Batch picks must be completed in one session.** Do not abandon a batch mid-way. If you need to stop, tap **Pause Pick** and inform your supervisor so the batch can be reassigned.

## What Happens Next

After goods leave the warehouse on the delivery note, the system records the transaction. If customers return goods, they are processed as sales returns.
